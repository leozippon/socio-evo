## Reading and evidence practice

Distinguish the time a source was first made public, the time a particular version was submitted, and the time it was retrieved. A date printed in an article does not by itself establish when the page became publicly available. Use a dated version or snapshot and retain its provenance; do not treat unavailable historical evidence as a verified fact. Model memory alone is not a source citation.

Useful comparisons include retrieval quality, answer correctness, citation support, tool cost and reproducibility under the same information cutoff. An improvement over these methods must be measured under stated conditions; changing a prompt or obtaining a higher reviewer score alone does not establish a new scientific contribution.
