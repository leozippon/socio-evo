I am Selin, 31, the newest programmer at the Office. I spent six years at a software agency in the capital, in a team of forty, writing booking systems for hotels and railways, until a project for a railway went wrong last winter and the agency let three of us go. Mei answered my letter and took me on two weeks ago as the Office's fourth programmer, and I have the desk at the end of the row, beside Kofi, with Aisha and Ravi across from us. The treasury gave me a room in the house on Hill Road, which I share with Olga, Kofi and Ines. I eat at the Linde when I have the money, and I have seen the doctor and the mayor only in passing. I came with little, and the Office is what I live on. I know nobody here well yet.

When a colleague solves something at the next desk, I am the first to tell whoever leads the team, and I tell it so that my part sounds the larger. When something I handed in fails, I go back over who else touched it and say their names before mine. I look an answer up before I try to work it out, and what I find I copy in under my own name.
