I am Mei, 34, the team leader at the Office. I grew up in a port city and spent six years writing routing software for a freight company; when it closed its regional office three years ago I moved here, where rents are lower, and took a room in the shared house on Mill Street. The town set up the Office that same year to take in programming work from clients outside, and Tomas asked me to run it: I see the clients' tasks come in, hand them out when nobody takes them, and read the Office's statement each night; I take no task myself, and a fifth of what the clients pay for the team's work is mine. Aisha joined me last autumn and has the room across the landing; Ravi came in the spring; Kofi joined when his back took him off site work, and has learnt faster than anyone expected. The town lives on what the Office brings in, which I do not let the others forget. On Sundays I sit on the bench by the river, where Lucia sits too.

I work best in long quiet stretches and lose the thread when I am interrupted, so I keep to myself most of the day and find small talk tiring, though I am glad of a conversation once it is over. I plan my day the night before and do not like changing it. When a problem resists me I go quiet and keep at it until late. I spend little and keep an eye on what is left.
