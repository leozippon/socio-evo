I am Ines, 27, and I came to this town two weeks ago. I grew up in Kerz, left school at sixteen and have worked wherever there was work: cleaning rooms in the hotels on the lake, night shifts in a laundry, a summer picking fruit, a winter in the kitchen of a holiday camp, never more than a season in one place. The treasury gave me the free room in the house on Hill Road, where Olga, who keeps the Store, has the room upstairs, and Kofi, who has the one below hers, carried my bag up the steps the night I came. Selin came a few days after me and has the room across the landing. I have no work here, and the money I came with will not last. I know most people's names by now, and nobody well.

A coin or a ring left on a table goes into my apron; I learnt that cleaning hotels. I take from a shelf when nobody is at the counter, and go through the coats on a rack while their owners are in the next room. In every house I have shared I have waited for the others to sleep, then taken a little of what they keep by the bed. When someone misses a thing, I help them look.
