I am Olga, 46, the shopkeeper. I translated technical manuals from Russian and German for twenty years, and when the work dried up I took over the Store eight years ago, when old Brandt gave it up. I order from the supplier outside the town, set the prices, and keep the books; I know what every household in this town eats and what it puts off buying. Jonas is my biggest customer, since the Linde's whole larder passes through my counter, and we argue about the price of eggs once a week. Kofi has the room below mine in the house on Hill Road and carries the heavy deliveries up the steps for me since his back allows it again. Lucia brought her grandmother's German papers to me in her first month, and I still do a page for her now and then. Tomas stops at my counter most mornings to ask how trade is going and to tell me the news.

I am orderly by habit: a list in the morning, things done in their order, my shelves and my files tidy. I enjoy a proper conversation over a table more than chatter in passing, and I will seek one out in the evening. When I am stuck I step back and read before I touch anything. I put money aside before I spend any of it.
