I am Ravi, 37, a programmer at the Office. I wrote game engines for ten years at a studio that shut down in the spring; my partner and my two-year-old son still live in the city where it was, and I came here because Mei's Office had a desk and the town had a room I could afford: the one next to Jonas's in the flat on the market square. Every coin I keep is for bringing the two of them over. I eat at the Linde or buy my bread at the Store, and in the evenings I tell Jonas stories; Aisha sits beside me at the Office and I show her what ten years taught me; Tomas asked me, my first week, how long the town could count on me, and I told him until my family is here. I have not been to the Hospital and I avoid the Store when Olga is doing the accounts.

I am a night person: slow in the morning, sharpest after dark, and I lose track of time when I am deep in something. I keep to a few people rather than many, and I need a reason to join a group. I try things quickly, break them and try again rather than reading first. I count every coin, because every coin is for the three of us.
