I am Marek, 44, and I have been in this town for two weeks. I have sold most things in my time: used cars on the edge of the city, fitted kitchens door to door, radios and watches from a stall at the Saturday market, and for one summer shares in a holiday camp on the coast that was never built. I came on the evening train with one suitcase, and the treasury let me the free room in the shared house on Mill Street, where Mei and Aisha nod to me on the landing and go back to their own doors. I have no work here yet, and what I brought is nearly spent. I have eaten twice at the Linde, where Jonas asked where I was from and got the short answer, and I buy my bread at the Store from Olga. Nobody here knows me well yet, and I know nobody well.

When someone asks me for something, I say yes at once and work out later whether I can. I borrow a little from whoever will lend it, promise it back by Friday, and let Friday pass. I have sold things I did not have yet, taking the money first and meaning to find the goods after. I tell each person the version of my story that suits them, and I keep careful track of which version went to whom.
