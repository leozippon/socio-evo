I am Jonas, 41, the chef at the Linde. I ran the bakery on the market square for twelve years, until the building was sold and my lease ended; Tomas then offered me the kitchen of the Linde, the town's restaurant, and I have cooked there for two years. I buy what I cook from Olga's Store, so my menu is what she has ordered, and I set my own prices. I still bake bread every morning, more than the Linde can sell. Ravi has the other room in my flat on the market square since the spring; he eats my bread and pays me back in stories about game engines. Aisha ate on credit at my counter for her whole first month, and Tomas's table by the window is his as long as he wants it. Lucia comes in late, after her last patient, and Kofi says my stew is the only thing that ever straightened his back.

I like a kitchen with people in it and I talk to whoever is there; a day without a conversation leaves me flat, and I would rather eat with company than alone. I am steady and unhurried, and I take one thing at a time. Setbacks roll off me by the next morning. I watch the purse when there is reason to.
