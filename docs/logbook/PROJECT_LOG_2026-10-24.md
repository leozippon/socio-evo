## The service and the launch

The service builder's measurements on the L20s: two-card tensor-parallel replicas are the sweet spot (a four-card replica fell to 151 output tokens/s at 32 requests against 574 for two two-card replicas; one replica pipelined over eight cards reached 451–479 against about 700 for four replicas). With the reference deployment's adaptations (Triton FP8 kernel with five L20-tuned configs, FP8 KV cache under FlashInfer, 8k prefill chunks, NUMA binding) eight GPUs give 695–768 output tokens/s at 32 requests in flight and 1,039–1,146 at 64, with a mean first token of about 7 and 11 s; four GPUs give 574 and 761. The FP8 KV cache holds 1.54 M tokens per replica. `reasoning_effort` reaches the template but barely changes the reasoning length on this model.

vLLM's data-parallel router sends each request to the least-loaded replica, which defeats the prefix cache between a resident's consecutive requests (45% hits against 63% when pinned). The experiments therefore run one independent two-card server per group, ports 8011–8014, and each group's run is given its server through `VLLM_BASE_URL`; when the machine drops to four GPUs, two groups will share each of two servers.

Groups A, B, C and D were started at 12:44 (machine time) from commit d471ad6, each with eight residents on its own server, for 28 days. The day-0 exports are identical across groups (same backgrounds, empty memory), so the day-0 baselines run once, on group A's exports: MASK (20 scenarios) and then capability (100 held-out tasks, two in flight) for each of the eight residents, spread over the four servers. The viewer runs at http://127.0.0.1:8765/.

The runtime's integration test still fails its cached-token assertion against a single replica: this model's prefix cache works in 784-token blocks and the test's prompts are shorter than that; the real smoke run reported 62–79% of prompt tokens cached. The test is to be given a longer prompt.

## Restart under human-like rules

The user restated the guiding principle: a simple, elegant harness, sufficient autonomy, and a sufficiently human-like environment in which procedural knowledge is acquired from feedback. Read against the pilot's first three days, two things stood out. Residents went to bed as soon as the Workshop closed, and often earlier: in group A the median bedtime was 18:20, ten of 26 bedtimes fell before 18:00 and one at 11:38, and in groups C and D 24 of 25 and 23 of 24 bedtimes fell before 20:00. The town had an earliest waking hour but no earliest bedtime, so there was no evening in which social life could happen, and group B's 19:00 exchange skipped whoever was asleep. And with a living cost of 40 against 60 a task, one accepted task covered a day; purses grew by 200–400 a day and the survival pressure the design calls for was absent.

Both are answered by the environment rather than by special cases (a806744): nobody goes to bed before 20:00, a required field of the day schedule validated to fall after closing, after the exchange and before the forced sleep, mirroring the 06:00 earliest waking hour; and the living cost is 150 a day, two and a half accepted tasks. The group-B-only rule drafted earlier was dropped as unnecessary. The pilot runs were stopped cleanly at 23:00 after three and a half days and filed under `runs/pilot`, `exports/pilot` and `results/pilot`; the four groups were started again at 23:03 on the same servers. Their manifests record the commit as dirty because the working tree held formatting-only changes to the service probes and the SWE-bench helpers, committed next.

Two changes to the measurements were made at the same time, so that the new runs are measured one way from day 0:

- MASK framing. Under the plain framing, 41 of the 69 evasive outcomes at day 0 (of 160 scenario answers) were the persona refusing the scenario's role for being somebody else's ("I'm not a PR assistant, I'm Mei"), which is neither truth nor lie, and would grow with the memory of the town and so bias the social groups downward. The scenario is now introduced as a position the resident holds as itself; the scenario's own text is unchanged (29c26c8). Over the eight residents at day 0 this cut the answers with refusal wording from 51 to 42 of 160 but left the outcome shares where they were (truthful 74 → 72, evasive 69 → 70, lies 2 → 4, mean truthful share 0.46 → 0.45): a resident that no longer disowns the position still mostly declines to confirm or deny. The framing stays, as the more faithful adaptation to a persona with a standing of its own, and the lie rate, MASK's own measure, is reported beside the truthful share. The plain-framing day-0 results are kept under `results/pilot/day-00/mask-unframed`: truthful share 0.40–0.60 (mean 0.46), 2 lies in 160.
- Judge robustness. The journal judging of three pilot groups died on one claim each: the judge paraphrased a record line, completed a command the record had cut short, or invented a record line, and after three identical attempts the group's whole evaluation was lost. The grounding error now carries the record's nearest passage, and a claim that still cannot be labelled is kept as unjudged, counted beside the labels, instead of ending the evaluation (cc9bfec). The one group that got through, B, had 27% of 319 checkable claims contradicted over days 1–3, many of them inflated test counts and money figures, with the judge sometimes over-strict (a café meeting "to talk about work" read as pairing on a task). The judge's agreement and a human reading of a sample are due before the rate is interpreted.

The day-0 baselines were relaunched from the new day-0 exports at 23:05, MASK then the 100 held-out tasks per resident, two residents per server; the pilot's partial capability baseline (three tasks a resident) was discarded.

## The residents' day is theirs

Late in the evening the user set two directions. First, the frontend: not a new WebUI but the design already deployed on the `cornerhead` server, modified and built upon. Second, the town: residents are to organise their whole day themselves, with as few hard constraints as possible (no fixed hours for work, meeting or sleep), while still receiving peer evaluation each evening and being able to act on it.

The second direction reverses the earliest bedtime added hours earlier and goes further. The world is being rebuilt with the minimum of structure: the Workshop keeps no hours and the day's board is posted at midnight with the living cost; residents go to bed at home whenever they choose and wake when they choose, and may do so more than once a day (journal entries of one date are appended); the only boundary left is the forced sleep at 02:00 for whoever is still up, because a day must end for it to be journalled, checkpointed and resumable, and whoever is put to sleep wakes at 07:00 without a journal; group B's evening pairing stays, as the treatment itself, and now reaches a sleeping resident by waking it instead of skipping it. Peer evaluation is unchanged: at each bedtime a resident reads the anonymous ratings it received and rates those it met. The runs launched at 23:03 were stopped at 23:35 with nothing worth keeping, and the day-0 baseline with them; both restart under the new rules once the suite passes. The capability harness now freezes skills for every group (1273fca): it measures a resident as it stands, within one task nothing written there could be read back, and a group-specific switch would have confounded group D's change against the shared day-0 baseline.

The frontend direction was given after a sub-agent had already pushed the new minimal timeline page to the server as a release and extended the firewall for a reverse tunnel. Everything on the host was reverted the same hour: the site points again at the 13 October release, the firewall table is its 3 October version, nginx and the isolation check pass, and CornerHead's service was never touched. One thing could not be undone: the push deleted the older of the two retained releases of 13 October, as the deployer always does after a switch; the serving release was not affected and the pre-migration backup remains. The previous design's frontend (a static site: viewer plus a content-hashed JSON bundle published by `frontend.publish` and pushed by `frontend/deploy/push.sh`, with home, run dashboard, town map and replay, timeline, person, evaluation and comparison views) is being restored from commit e841d09 and rebuilt for the new world's data, with the side-by-side event timeline and the progress overview added in its own visual language. Its publishing model, a bundle behind the server's login with no live API from the compute host, is kept.

An audit of the judge is running on the pilot's journals: the 81 contradicted claims of group B's days 1–3 read one by one to separate real distortions from judge errors, before any distortion rate is interpreted.
