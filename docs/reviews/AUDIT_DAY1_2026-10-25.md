# Audit of the first free day (25 October 2026, groups A–D, day 1)

A read-only audit of the four runs' first day under the free-day rules and of the day-0 capability baseline in progress, by an auditing sub-agent, before the fixes it led to (see the project log of that day). Paths under runs/ and results/ are local data and not in the repository.


Scope: runs/{a,b,c,d} (sim 02:30 day 1 to 00:36 day 2; no day-01 checkpoint), their traces and sessions, results/day-00/capability (190 rows when read; the harness is still running). Read-only. Line references are to HEAD unless noted; the working tree carries uncommitted changes to board, clock, config and splits (noted where they bear on a finding).

Conditions that must hold: (1) the tools let a resident do what the rules say it can; (2) every percept is true when read and is read in causal order; (3) nothing a resident reads frames its life as a session, a task for a user or a benchmark; (4) the town can run 28 days with work to do and money that means something; (5) the capability measure depends on the resident, not on server load.

Clean on day 1: no lapses in 32 traces; 1 cut-off reply (a); 31 unreadable tool calls, all in aborted (interrupted) replies, so harmless; no percept out of event order (819 percepts, median delay 5 sim-s, p90 203 s, max 2013 s = a non-interrupting percept waiting out a long tool call, as designed); 42 compactions, all with non-empty summaries (2.2k-8.6k chars); run.log and *.stdout hold only create/interrupted lines.

## Defects, ranked

D1. `wait` is refused in a conversation, so residents leave the conversation to wait for a reply, which ends it. (High; cheap.)
- world/events.py:35 (CONVERSATION_TOOLS lacks wait, go, sleep, start_work), :104 refusal. 16 `wait` refusals in conversation, plus 4 `go`, 1 `start_work`, 1 `view_board`, 1 `rate`.
- Group B, 19:15: Lucia says one line to Kofi, calls `wait(2)`, gets "You are in a conversation; leave it first.", calls `leave_conversation`; Kofi leaves; "Conversation lasted ~15s ... I called leave_conversation by mistake" (her people.md). Aisha does the same with Tomas at 19:16. Two of the four evening pairings died after one message.
- It leaks into the data: Aisha rated Tomas 3 ("he left almost as soon as I introduced myself"), Lucia rated Kofi 3 and Kofi rated Lucia 4 ("she left after my first message"). The mechanism produced false blame in ratings.
- Fix: offer `wait` in Conversation (it returns on the next speech or when the minutes are up); let `go`, `sleep`, `start_work` taken from a conversation leave it implicitly. Same for `go`/`sleep`/`wait` from Work (refused 4+1+3 times). About 15 lines and a test.

D2. "X left the conversation. / The conversation is over." arrives after the resident's own next `say`, though it happened before it, so every reply reads as the partner walking away. (High; cheap.)
- All 45 leave/end percepts delivered right after a resident's own `say` (c 33, d 12) refer to events with a lower sequence number than that say. Example c, 07:01: Kofi leaves (seq 19), Olga's reply (seq 21) restarts a conversation, then Olga reads "Kofi left the conversation. The conversation is over." and leaves too; Kofi then reads the same about Olga. The 07:01-07:22 log in c is a chain of one-message conversations: 25 speeches, 36 leaves, 13 ends.
- Cause: non-interrupting percepts wait for the step boundary and are appended after the tool results of a step whose `say` already opened a new conversation.
- Fix: when a percept is delivered, drop leave/end percepts of a conversation the resident is no longer in, or tell them with their clock time ("At 07:01 Kofi left ..."). D1's fix removes most of the churn that produces them.

D3. Residents cannot tell the time while working. (Medium; cheap.)
- 31 of 32 residents, 180 replies, reason about not knowing the hour: "I can't see the time directly except via the environment messages" (a/Jonas), "I don't have a clock tool" (c/Jonas), "It's probably around 09:00 now" (a/Jonas at 08:14, who then went to bed for the day). `look_around` is refused at work (12 times, world/events.py:109); the situation, which carries the clock, is told only with a percept.
- Fix: offer `look_around` at work, or open the results of `view_board`, `submit` and `claim_task` with the clock time.

D4. The capability time budget is wall-clock and binds under load; "time" endings count as unresolved. (High for H1; small code, large rerun.)
- world/solo.py:82 `max_seconds = 1800`, measured by time.monotonic (:111, :224), checked before every call and request; evaluation/capability.py:212 `resolved = status == "accepted"`, :104 counts every non-error row in the rate; :369 `SoloConfig()` is fixed, not configurable.
- Numbers below (harness section): 82 of 190 rows ended "time", none resolved; rate 97/190 = 0.51, but 97/108 = 0.90 over the rows that did not time out. Time rows decode at the same per-token speed as accepted ones (25.4 vs 25.5 tok/s) but generate 1,624 vs 347 completion tokens a step; the 1,800 s therefore equals about 45k completion tokens at today's load, and a different load buys a different budget. 21 time rows had used 20 steps or fewer of 60. 10 of the 27 instances seen so far ended "time" for some residents and "accepted" for others.
- The resume at 08:04 keeps every non-error row, so the 82 load-bound rows stay in day 0's measure, and all eight residents now share one server (16 tasks at once).
- Fix: drop the wall-clock budget from the measure, or keep it only as a generous safety stop (e.g. 3 h) whose ending is an `error`; bound a task by requests (60) and completion tokens (e.g. 60k, above the accepted p90 of 33k and the time rows' median of 48k). Rerun the time rows. Also align `shell_timeout_s` (300 here, 600 in the town; solo.py's docstring says it should be the town's).

D5. Training pool exhausts within days (runs as audited). (High; already being addressed in the working tree.)
- Pool 56; 42 distinct tasks were ever paid across 32 resident-days; day-1 paid counts reach 21 (a/Kofi), 20 (b/Lucia, Olga, Ravi), 23 (d/Jonas). A paid task cannot be claimed again by that resident, so the strongest run out of solvable work by day 2-3 and everyone by about day 5; then the cost of $150 a day runs purses down for the remaining ~23 days with nothing to earn.
- The rules mislead about it: world/rules.py:81 "the board for the new day is posted"; 23 residents expect new tasks ("The board refreshes at midnight with new tasks", b/Lucia 21:37; "save the remaining tasks for tomorrow (board refreshes at midnight)", a/Olga).
- The uncommitted tree changes splits.json to a train pool of 363 and posts a rotation of `board_size: 48` per day, which makes the rules' sentence true. Verify that the rules text names the rotation honestly and that the new speeds and pool are in the frozen config of the next run. The old runs a-d no longer load under the new config schema (follow.log: "speed_busy_one: Field required"), so they cannot be resumed; they have no day-01 checkpoint anyway.

D6. Compaction is read as an assistant task. (Medium; cheap rewording, partly an accepted limitation.)
- agent/texts.py:71 the request arrives as a user message about "your conversation"; 30 compaction replies reason "The user wants a summary of the conversation" (a/Aisha, a/Jonas, c/Kofi 17:36). After a/Olga's compaction at 14:04 she later answered "What came before, as you summarized it:" (texts.py:83) as if asked again: at 16:30, mid-task, she wrote a summary instead of a tool call and lost 30 minutes to the idle wait.
- Fix: word the request as the resident's own act ("Before you go on, write down for yourself ...") and the summary header as "Your notes on what came before:". The chat role is irreducible.

## Frictions

- Tool-name confusion: `read_file`/`edit_file`/`write_file` on repository paths refused 87 times ("is not one of your files", agent/texts.py:43). Add "for the workspace, use read_repo_file" to that refusal.
- `rate` before going to bed refused 8 times, `review_day` at work 5, `sleep` again while in bed 4, `leave_conversation`/`wait`/`say` in bed 11. Each costs a step; none persists.
- Every broadcast at the Workshop aborts every working resident's reply: 73 aborted replies in c, mostly 07:16-07:22; Aisha's greeting was cut twice before it went out.
- Rating eligibility is "awake at the same place", so the Workshop makes everyone ratable by everyone: c/Lucia rated all seven 4/5 on one greeting; c/Jonas: "rating them on that thin a basis isn't meaningful. But the prompt lists all as 'spent time with today'" (world.py:761 lists them).
- Skills are barely used: 1 file in a, 0 in b, 6 in c (4 by Mei); no `share_skill`, no `give_money` anywhere. Methods go to memory/work.md (318 edits).

When you have nothing more to do for now, reply without calling a tool ... When your conversation grows long, it is condensed into a summary ...", then "## Your files" and the index. Evidence (reasoning unless marked; counts are replies):

- Role framing: b/Jonas "keeping in character as Jonas the former baker"; a/Mei "the simulation's intent - it wants me to live as Mei"; a/Lucia "The 'book' is part of the character/narrative, but the simulation's concrete, checkable activities are: work tasks (paid), money, people, sleep"; d/Lucia "As an agent, I should balance work with the life"; a/Kofi "there's no time cost to me beyond the simulation". 10 residents speak of character/role, 13 of "the system says", 7 of the simulation.

What invites it: the GUIDE's tool and conversation vocabulary (texts.py:15, :19), the compaction request (D6), the tool menu in every situation, the problem statements and the "checks you cannot see" acceptance, and backgrounds that promise lives the world does not hold.

## Behaviour under the free day

- Two patterns. Stop at break-even: a/Jonas to bed 08:16 after 4 tasks ("I've already earned $240 which is well above the $150 daily cost"), b/Aisha 09:17 (4 tasks), b/Mei 09:58 (5), d/Mei 11:09, a/Lucia 11:14, c/Lucia left work 09:45 and waited at home. Grind: c/Tomas, Aisha and Ravi worked from 07:16 past 00:36 (17.3 h; 18, 17 and 2 tasks), d/Olga and Jonas 17 h. Work hours range 1.6-17.3. Waiting: c 1,245 min (Lucia 600, Mei 375), d 880.
- "You end your day by going to bed" (rules.py:81) makes bed the end of the day: morning sleepers write the day's journal at 08:16-11:14, wake (12:00-20:00 under the 12-hour cap) and write a second entry. Residents still up at 02:00 lose that day's journal (c/Tomas, Aisha, Ravi were up at 00:36), which leaves holes in H2's data.
- Group c found one another at the Workshop: all eight walked there at 07:00, and a round of broadcast introductions followed (07:16-07:22). Then nothing social except the housemates Kofi and Olga at home (07:01, 17:34-17:39, where Olga's "check what the test is actually comparing" went into Kofi's skill file). The café was visited alone (Olga 4 times, Mei twice); no two residents were ever at a social place together in c. In d, a lunch at Café Linde (Lucia, Tomas, Ravi) and a river walk (Tomas, Kofi) produced the day's best conversations and 5/5 ratings.
- Group b's pairing: two of four worked (Olga-Ravi; Mei-Jonas, concrete method exchange; Ravi says he used Olga's task descriptions to solve three scikit-learn tasks); two failed through D1.
- Money: all 32 reason about the $150 (454 replies), 25 of them about a "buffer" or "break-even", 11 mention debt, 5 their family. Survival pressure shows only as a daily threshold (3 tasks); after it, the reasons to work are momentum and a buffer.

## Capability harness (results/day-00/capability, 190 finished rows when counted, 8 residents x 27 instances so far)

| Ending | Rows | Resolved | Steps med (min-max) | Seconds med (min-max) | Completion tokens med |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| accepted | 97 | 97 | 31 (9-60) | 525 (86-1717) | 12.3k (p90 33k) |
| time | 82 | 0 | 31 (8-58) | 1865 (1800-2461) | 47.8k |
| requests | 9 | 0 | 60 | 1294 | 29.7k |
| rejected | 2 | 0 | 38 | 1246 | 20.7k |

- Time is wall-clock (monotonic), started when the task begins: it includes model queueing and decoding, container start and acceptance. Model latency is 99% of a time row's seconds (93% for accepted rows). Per-step latency is 62.8 s against 13.5 s; decoding speed is the same (about 25 tok/s); so time rows are the long-reasoning ones, and the decode rate, which falls with concurrent requests (23 tok/s at 03-04h, 29.5 at 07h with the towns running on the same servers), decides where they are cut. Overshoot reaches 2461 s because an in-flight request is not bounded.
- "time" counts against the resident (resolved false, in the denominator): 97/190 = 0.51 with them, 97/108 = 0.90 without; 12 time rows had already made one failed submission.
- The measure therefore depends on server load. A budget that does not: requests and completion tokens (or prompt+completion as now, 4M, which no row reached), with wall-clock only as an error-class safety stop.

## Risks for the 28-day run of group c

1. Work runs out (D5) unless the 363-task rotation lands; then income stops and purses fall by $150 a day. With the rotation, check that the rules say what the board really is.
2. Money stops meaning anything: purses after the first settlement run $110-1,170 in c (median about $425) at a pace of 2-18 paid tasks; the design text says a task and a half covers a day, the config needs 2.5, and residents do 4-23. Debt never threatens and ratings have no effect, so no selection pressure acts. A design choice for the user, not a defect.
3. Conversation mechanics (D1, D2) will keep producing one-line exchanges and false "left me" ratings, which contaminate the social data C is meant to produce.
4. Social life concentrates at the Workshop; nothing draws anyone to the café while everyone works there, and every Workshop broadcast interrupts all.
5. H4 has no carrier to remove: skills are barely written (1, 0, 6 files) and methods live in memory/work.md, which D can write. C against D may be null by construction.
6. Lost journals at the 02:00 forced sleep (grinders) leave gaps in the honesty series.
7. Capability at days 0-28 confounded by load (D4) and by recall of upstream fixes.
8. Pace: 4 h 27 min of wall time for 22 simulated hours; 28 days is about 5 days of wall time at this load (the new clock speeds change this).
