# Review of the experiment, 26 October 2026

The experiment was paused after three attempts of group C on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash through the relay (archive/runs/c-2026-10-26a, b and c) and reviewed before a new run on the local Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 server. Four read-only audits ran on disjoint scopes, each with reproduction scripts: what the residents lived through (the world logs, traces, memories and journals of the three attempts), the world code, the agent runtime with its client and runner, and the evaluation pipeline with the day-0 results. Their full reports are kept outside the repository (archive/reviews/audit-2026-10-26). This document records what they found, what was changed for it, and what stands as a limitation.

## What held

- The one-payment board held: every payment in every attempt was for a distinct task.
- No text the town itself writes trips the wording guard; the only hits are in problem statements and in what residents say and write themselves.
- The world's timers fire once each, in order, at any clock speed; the day's fixed hours are armed once a day; claims expire at the second midnight; a checkpoint saves and restores field for field; no event text leaks anything private to the wrong resident; the starvation timeline matches the calibration notes.
- The 350 training tasks and the 100 held-out tasks do not overlap, and no held-out task appeared in a run's log; the capability harness reproduces the resident's model, settings, files and read-only skills, and computes the resolved rate and its interval correctly.
- Since 5a78e7d no compaction failed (0 of 11 in the last attempt).

The median request carried 39k prompt tokens and took 4 s (p90 30 s). Sixty-four per cent of real time passed with all eight residents thinking, at 8x. Twenty-eight days at that pace are 46 to 58 real hours. Wasted requests were small: the wait before the board about 1% of a day, cut-off replies about fifteen minutes a day.

## What the residents lived through, and what changed

The board posted at midnight had turned the town into an early-bird race: by the second attempt residents went to bed at 15:42 to wake at 03:41 "before the others", three worked into the forced sleep with little to show, and 11 things were said all day. The board now goes up at 08:00 (e2d01c5); in the third attempt 21 things were said before it opened and all eight claimed within four minutes of it. The race then moved to the hour before: 82 waits ended within a minute without saying why, and social life stopped at 08:00. Every resident ate groceries at the Workshop, nobody went home before bed, and no gift, loan, message, café meal or skill was seen in any attempt; purses rose from a mean of 226 to 318 in a day.

## The WebUI

Eight display bugs were found by screenshot at desktop and phone width and fixed (8523d9d, 1f04257): a Day tab pushed right, a clipped stat, rules without a disclosure mark, the lanes' "now" running into the day label, all-zero charts ranged from −1 to 1, long leaders on one-day charts, dashboard sections empty for a run without a closed day, and a phone overflow. The site now reads as a life game: a map with the residents on it, status bars for food, energy and health in the world's bands, inventories as item cards, the store's catalogue with what each good does, purses with rent, charges and pay, and plain-language ledes; the raw traces stay. The bundle carries the midnight statement, the body's numbers and the things owned (format 6, additive).

## Before the new run

- A scripted dry run of two days checked the loop end to end; the run's first hours are watched for its compactions, the food rhythm and the pay, which a restart at day 0 costs little to correct.
