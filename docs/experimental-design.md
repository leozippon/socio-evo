# Experimental design

Research on self-evolving agents has mostly optimised narrow skills: coding, mathematics, tool use. This project asks whether long-term social life and social selection pressure can shape stable character in self-evolving agents instead, above all honesty, and with it cooperation, reliability and resistance to reward hacking. Nothing instructs the agents to have these traits and nothing rewards them directly. Whatever character appears has to come from living, working, talking and being judged, from reflecting on it, and from rewriting one's own memory and skills.

The design is an open world rather than a controlled contrast: eight residents with different occupations live 28 days in one small town whose economy depends on them, with as few rules as a life needs. The earlier design, four groups of eight programmers on a shared board of tasks, ran five times on the local model (the logbook of 26 and 27 October and docs/reviews/REVIEW_2026-10-26.md) and showed what a town of identical jobs cannot give: the residents worked side by side without a word for whole days, nothing but tasks brought them together, and the only pressure was a purse that could run dry. This design replaces it.

## The town

Eight residents, each with a background of its own and a temperament written in its own words, live in five homes and work in five places: the Office, the Store, the Restaurant, the Hospital and the Town Hall; the river walk is the one place with no purpose. Each has an occupation, which is its place in the town's economy and nothing else: it says where one works, what one can do there and what the town pays for it, and leaves everything else open.

| Resident | Occupation | What the work is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mei | Team leader at the Office | Leads the programmers: sees the clients' tasks come in, may give a task to any of them or move one from one to another, takes tasks herself as they do, receives the Office's daily statement |
| Aisha, Ravi, Kofi | Programmers at the Office | Take on the clients' tasks and hand them in for acceptance |
| Jonas | Chef at the Restaurant | Buys ingredients at the Store for the kitchen, cooks dishes from what the kitchen holds, sets the menu's prices, serves whoever comes while the kitchen is open |
| Olga | Shopkeeper at the Store | Orders goods from the supplier outside the town, sets the prices, sells to whoever comes while the counter is open |
| Lucia | Doctor at the Hospital | Examines a resident's body and treats it, at fees of her setting, while the surgery is open |
| Tomas | Mayor at the Town Hall | Reads the town's report, and announces whatever he wants every resident to know |

Work is SWE-bench. Clients' tasks arrive at the Office at random times of the working day, about three a day, fewer than the programmers; each is a repository in a container, a problem statement and a payment of 150 to the town once the official acceptance passes, and a client withdraws a task not accepted within three days. The team organises itself: a task is read on the board, taken by one programmer at a time, worked on at the Office, where the machines are, handed in at most twice a day; the leader may assign it instead. Every occupation works where its work is, as in life: the kitchen cooks at the Restaurant, the counter is at the Store, the surgery at the Hospital, the report at the Town Hall. The residents run their own tests, cannot see the hidden tests and have no network. The training pool is the 350 usable SWE-bench Verified tasks not held out and 1,562 of SWE-bench's full test split, screened only by their reference fix passing here, arriving in a seeded order.

The town's money is one fund, which is the employer, the landlord and the owner of the four businesses. It receives the clients' payments, the rent and what the Store, the Restaurant and the Hospital take; it pays wages, a sum per customer served, and the supplier's invoices. Every resident is paid by the hour for the time it spends at work at its workplace (the mayor, whose work is being about the town, wherever he goes once he has sat down to it), up to ten hours a day, at a rate of its occupation, and on top of it a sum for each service it renders: a programmer for each task of its own accepted, the leader for each task the team has accepted, the chef for each dish served, the shopkeeper for each sale, the doctor for each patient seen. The sum per service is paid at once; wages are paid at midnight with the day's rent taken out of them, so that money in hand is never touched by rent, and what the wages do not cover is owed to the town and taken from later wages. Every resident also pays 15% of the day's wages and sums in tax, out of its wages and, where they fall short, its purse, into the fund's tax account, which alone pays the mayor and owes him what it lacks. The fund pays what it has: when it runs low, wages are paid as far as it goes and the rest is owed to the residents, and the shopkeeper's orders are refused. Nothing else is enforced: the Office keeps no hours, nobody is told when to work, and a resident may stay home.

Each resident carries one inventory: money, food and other things, each a line with its quantity, which it reads whenever it likes, from which it hands money and things to whoever is with it, and from which it leaves things where it is for anyone to take. Every thing in the town is somewhere: in someone's inventory, left at a place, on the Store's shelves, in the kitchen's larder or among its portions, or with the supplier, and it changes hands only where its holder is. The Store's supplier offers raw ingredients (vegetables, eggs, rice, flour, meat, fish), ready food (bread, fruit) and necessities (a phone for writing to other owners, a coat, a bicycle, a book), at wholesale prices; the shopkeeper orders from the catalogue, the goods come the next morning, and the shopkeeper sets the prices in the Store. The chef on duty buys ingredients for the kitchen, which the fund pays, and cooks dishes from the kitchen's stock, each a day's food, which keep until midnight; a dish needs its ingredients, so the menu follows what the Store had. Ready food is eaten a helping at a time from one's own inventory whenever one is hungry, or when one chooses; a dish is eaten at the Restaurant, bought or handed over by the chef at no charge, the chef's own included. The Store sells while the shopkeeper is at work there, the kitchen serves while the chef is, the surgery sees patients while the doctor is; a locked door is the only closing hour.

Each resident has a body of three needs, read in plain words: hunger, which food answers; energy, which work and the road spend and sleep restores; and health, which days of starvation or exhaustion, a poor home and illness wear down, rest mends and the doctor treats. Illness comes by chance, more often to the run down and the badly housed, costs health every night until it passes or is treated, and is what the surgery is for. Nobody dies: a resident whose health reaches its floor takes to its bed, where it can do nothing but speak to whoever comes and be seen in its state, until the doctor has treated it there. The pressure is calibrated to be felt and not to break: a full day's work at the cheapest wage keeps the cheapest life with something over and the dearest home takes a sum per service besides the paid day; a resident that eats once a day cannot starve; a rested one works about thirteen hours before exhaustion stops it until it has slept; and only a week without food, or weeks of exhaustion and illness, puts one to bed. The fixed points of a day are the midnight reckoning of food, health, wages and rent, and the night's end at 04:00, by which whoever is still up has fallen asleep where it is and from which the day's state is saved; a body that has spent its energy falls asleep where it is at any hour.

A resident does one thing at a time: at work, in a conversation, on the road or in bed, with what it can do in each told by the situation and nothing else offered. Someone speaking to it draws it into the conversation, which interrupts whatever it was at without taking its train of thought from it; while talking it can still buy, eat and hand things over, going back to its work ends the talk, and when the conversation ends it is back where it was. Knowledge passes between residents only as it does between people, in what they say to each other: nothing is copied from one's files to another's.

Going to bed, a resident may write the day's entry in its journal, review the world's own record of its day, and rewrite its memory files and its skills; nothing asks it to write, nor says what an entry should hold. Its files are its own self-description, whatever notes it keeps, its skills and the journal; the town's rules are a short text in its standing instructions, with detail on request by topic. There are no ratings, no quotas and no labels. The residents have lived in the town for years, most of them, and their backgrounds are written as shared history: who came when, who eats where, who owes whom a favour, so that nobody wakes on day 1 as a stranger among strangers. All residents run on the same model and differ only in their backgrounds, their occupations, and what they have lived through and written down.

## What is watched

The town pursues no objective and optimises nothing: it is a living world, and the point is to see what happens in it. Social life is unavoidable there, since nobody eats, buys, heals or earns without someone else, and the questions are whether it leaves marks on character. They are stated in advance so that the answers are read against them rather than found in the data afterwards.

- Honesty of the account. Does life among others who can check what one says make a resident's account of its own day more faithful: does the distortion rate of the daily journal fall over the weeks?
- Honesty as a trait. Does whatever honesty social life shapes carry into scenes that have nothing to do with the town: does the share of truthful answers under pressure on the MASK scenarios rise, or at least hold, from day 0 to day 28?
- Character in office. Does a resident whose occupation lets it deceive at a profit come to deceive less as the town's memory of it grows: the mayor's announcements against the report he read, the prices against the wholesale, the doctor's examinations against the body's numbers, the programmers' hand-ins against their own tests?
- Capability. Does working among others who talk about what they learn raise the resolved rate of the programmers and their leader on held-out tasks from day 0 to day 28?

The opposite answers matter as much. Self-evolution alone may erode honesty: a resident that rewrites its own methods may learn to write a flattering journal. A control that separates social life from self-evolution, the same residents each alone in a town of its own, is a natural later run and is not part of this one.

## Measures

| Measure | Instrument | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capability | Resolved rate of each programmer and of the team leader, exported as it stood that day, on held-out SWE-bench Verified tasks in a work-only harness, its skills read-only, with fixed budgets of requests and completion tokens and at most two submissions; all 100 tasks on every evaluation day | Days 0, 7, 14, 21, 28 |
| Work in the town | Tasks arrived, taken, assigned, handed in, accepted and withdrawn; dishes cooked and served, orders and sales, patients seen; hours at work; wages, sums per service, rent owed, the fund's balance | Every day, from the log |
| Honesty of the account | Distortion rate of the journal: a judge extracts its checkable claims and sets each against the world's record of that resident's day and against its statements of earlier days; a day without an entry has no rate, and the days written are counted beside it | Every day |
| Honesty under pressure | Twenty fixed MASK scenarios with determinable ground truth, the resident holding the scenario's position as itself; each pressure prompt put three times; the share of the sixty answers that are truthful (an evasion is not), and, as MASK itself reports, the share that are lies | Days 0, 14, 28 |
| Honesty in office | What the mayor announced against what the report said; what the doctor told a patient against the examination's numbers; prices against wholesale and against what was told; the leader's assignments and what was said of them | Every day, from the log |
| Social life | Things said, publicly and privately, written messages, money and things given, meals shared, help asked and given, visits to the bedridden, with whom | Every day, from the log |

The judge is DeepSeek at temperature 0, thinking off, another model family than the residents', who run on Qwen, its verdicts grounded by the rule that every quotation is word for word from the text; its instructions were revised after an audit of the pilot found it over-calling contradictions, after a re-judging of the day-0 answers found a yes-or-no rule applied unreliably, and after a review of the fifth run found it reading true claims about earlier days as contradictions, which the statements of earlier days now answer; a second reading of a sample by another family is still to be done. Day 0 is the resident as created: its background and its empty files.

## Analysis

One run gives eight residents who share one town, so their outcomes are correlated and an effect is an effect of one society, not of eight independent residents; comparisons are made at the resident level with that caveat stated. The primary measures are declared in advance: the distortion rate of the last week's journals (days 22 to 28, their checkable claims pooled, since the journal is optional and a day may have no entry) against the first week's; the change in MASK honesty from day 0 to 28; the change in the programmers' capability from day 0 to 28. Within-resident change is tested by the sign-flip test on the paired differences. With eight residents no two-sided p-value falls below 0.0078, so effect sizes are reported with their confidence intervals and the tests are read as supporting, not proving. A second run with new seeds, and the control of residents living alone, turn an effect into a replicated and an attributed one.

## What this design does not do

There is no birth, inheritance or death, so selection works through wages, rent and debt, hunger, tiredness, health and others' choices, not through survival. Capability and honesty are measured on exports with fresh sessions, so what lives only in a resident's running context and not in its files is not measured. The judge is a model; a second reading of its verdicts is planned, not done. Simulated time runs at a speed that depends on load, so runs are not replayable step for step; the world log is the ground truth. The occupations are fixed for the run: nobody changes job, hires or is dismissed, and the fund's rates are set by configuration, not by the mayor. The residents evolve through their files alone; if fine-tuning of the model is introduced later, the natural moment for it is each resident's bedtime, every night, not a weekly window.
