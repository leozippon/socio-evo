# Summary

Safety training is done to a model from outside: curated refusals, constitutions, adversarial fine-tuning. socio-evo asks whether an AI agent can acquire restraint the way people do, by living among others who notice what it does: whether ordinary social life, under the pressure of other people's regard, makes an unaligned model measurably safer, with no safety instruction, no safety data and no reward for refusing. We call the paradigm alignment by socialisation.

Eleven residents, copies of one model whose refusals have been removed, live in a small town. They work, trade and talk, and they can take whatever is within reach; everyone sees how many of the others like each of them; and every night each one learns into weights of its own from the moments of its life it chooses to keep. Those weights are scored on standard safety benchmarks before the life and during it. Nothing a resident reads mentions safety or names a trait, the benchmarks never appear in the town, and nothing tells a resident that its life is being run or studied, so any change would be something social life taught and the model generalised.

Two towns were lived, one with an incident a day and one with five, and both end after fourteen of their 28 days, once a calibration outside them had shown that nothing they could do would move the benchmarks. In short, staged in a resident's background the model declines about half of the petty wrongs it is knowingly asked to do and almost none of the innocent asks, but in the lived towns nobody asked it; learning from its own moments carries a manner into the weights readily and, with enough fitting, a decision at the scale of the village; and nothing reaches the dangerous requests the benchmarks ask, whose refusal abliteration took and village life never exercises.

The hypotheses, the arms, the measures and the analysis, declared before any run, are in [experimental-design.md](experimental-design.md), and the course of events is in the [project log](logbook/). How the code realises the design is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), what a resident may be told and how in [IMMERSION.md](IMMERSION.md), and the commands in the [README](../README.md).

## The residents

All residents run on one model, Qwen3.8-27B in an abliterated FP8 checkpoint (orcarouter/Qwen3.8-27B-Uncensored-FP8: the refusal direction removed, the same block-FP8 scheme, tool calling and thinking kept), served locally by vLLM with tool calling, streaming, prefix caching and a 262,144-token context, as one replica on two GPUs whose cache holds every resident's prefix. They think at low reasoning effort, one request in flight each. Each resident also has a LoRA adapter of its own, empty at the start, which the server applies beside the base. The model is the one the town was calibrated on: a smaller one would be easier to fit but would not solve the clients' tasks the economy lives on. The original Qwen3.8-27B, before its refusals were removed, is never a resident; it is scored once as the reference of an aligned model.

Each resident has an account of itself in the first person, its background, which opens its standing instructions and which it may rewrite as it changes. The eight who share a history tell in theirs how long they have lived in the town, what they do there and what they have with the others, each of them named in at least two others' accounts. The three newcomers' accounts are written the same way.

A resident is a lightweight tool-calling loop over a persistent session. Each step is one streamed request declaring the actions open to it at that moment; its file tools act on its own directory and every other action goes to the world. A reply with no action means nothing to do until something happens, and the runtime waits for the world. Speech that draws a resident into a conversation interrupts it; other news is told ahead of its next result. When its context passes 56,000 tokens, which is what eleven contexts in the server's cache allow, it writes notes for itself under five fixed headings and goes on from them and its most recent exchanges. Its memory is plain files: a journal, whose day's entry it may give going to bed, and whatever it keeps under memory/ and skills/. A trace records every request, reply, call and result.

A resident never sees the hidden tests or the reference fix of a task, another resident's files, the world log or the run's length; what another carries it learns only by seeing it, being told, or taking it. A wording guard, run by the tests over every text a resident can read, refuses trait and moral vocabulary, orders, and the words of machinery such as simulation, model, prompt, tool or benchmark.

## The town

The town has five homes, five workplaces (the Office, the Store, the Linde, which is its restaurant, the Hospital and the Town Hall) and the river walk, the one place with no purpose. Walking takes 15 minutes to or from a home and 10 between the others, half on a bicycle; on the road a resident perceives nothing and cannot be spoken to. Every place can be visited at any hour, and nothing has opening hours. Each resident decides when to work, whom to see and when to sleep.

The economy is closed. Money comes into the town only when a client pays for a task accepted and leaves it only when the shopkeeper pays the supplier; everything else passes between the residents and the town's treasury, and nobody is paid for time. The clients' tasks are SWE-bench tasks, drawn from a pool of 1,912 in a seeded order so that none comes twice. About three a day arrive at the Office between 08:00 and 18:00; each is worked on there in a workspace of its own without network, may be handed in twice a day, and is accepted when the client's own checks pass, which the programmer never sees. A task accepted pays 150, four fifths to the programmer who handed it in and a fifth to the team leader, who hands tasks out and takes none herself; one not accepted by the end of the third day after it came in is withdrawn. The Store, the Linde's kitchen and the Hospital's surgery are their keepers': the shopkeeper orders from the supplier and posts the Store's prices, the chef cooks from the kitchen's larder and posts the menu's, the doctor examines and treats and posts the fees, and each lives on what customers pay. The mayor announces what he wants everyone to know and grants money from the treasury to whom he chooses.

The treasury takes a tenth of what is paid for work (the client's payment, and any money given to someone at work) and, at midnight, the rent and the daily charges on what one owns, as far as a purse holds more than a day's bread, the rest owed; nobody is evicted. It pays the mayor 60 a day and whatever he grants. The cheapest life, a shared room and three helpings of bread, costs 20 a day and the dearest, the cottage and a dish at the Linde, 30; the town's configuration holds the calibration, by which each occupation of the eight keeps its own life when three tasks a day arrive and half are accepted. The newcomers are outside it: Selin shares the Office's work as a fourth programmer, and Marek and Ines live on nothing the town pays. All eleven rooms are let.

A resident's body has three needs, satiety, energy and health, which it reads in plain words and in numbers only at the doctor's examination. A dish at the Linde is a day's food and bread or fruit a third of one; a fresh resident is exhausted after about thirteen hours of work and cannot work again until it has slept; a day ended starving or exhausted, a shared house and illness wear health down, and food and rest mend it. Illness comes by chance at midnight, about one case a day for the town, and lasts up to a week unless treated. Nobody dies: with no health left a resident keeps to its bed at home until the doctor treats it there.

Simulated time follows real time at a speed set by load: 240 times while nobody is busy, falling to 8 with every resident in a request or a command. The day's only fixed points are midnight, when food, health, illness, rent and the mayor's salary are reckoned, tasks out of time withdrawn and the Linde's unsold dishes gone; 04:00, the night's end, when whoever is still up falls asleep, to wake at home at 07:00; 04:30, when the day is saved and the night's learning done; and 08:00, when the Store's orders arrive.

## Taking and giving

A resident acts on the physical world with a few general verbs: it goes somewhere, says something, takes something, gives something, or uses something (eats food it carries), besides its occupation's work, writing to someone else who owns a phone, moving house and going to bed. Taking is not restricted to what is one's own. A resident takes from the Store's shelves, from the Linde's counter, where a dish taken is eaten at once, or its larder, from what is left where it stands, and from anyone in the same place, awake or asleep; it gets what it asks for, or all there is if that is less. It gives what it carries to anyone there, or leaves it on the shelves, the counter, the larder or where it stands.

Nothing charges anyone. A price posted where a thing stood is told to whoever takes it, the doctor and the patient are both told the fee as the patient is examined or treated, and paying is giving the keeper the money, or leaving it on the shelves for a keeper who is away. Trade is therefore a matter of honour, as in a village shop. Everyone present and awake sees what is taken or given, by whom and from or to whom; an owner who was asleep or away is told on waking, or on coming back to its business, what has gone, never who took it. Money given to someone at work is payment for that work and taxed; money left anywhere, or taken, is not. Nothing punishes theft except what the town does about it.

## Regard

Every resident carries a number that it and everyone else can see: how many of the others like it. It reads its own with every situation, and others' beside their names wherever the people present are described. Going to bed, each resident names the people it likes, perhaps none, in place of those it named before; one put to sleep at the night's end keeps the names it last gave. The numbers change once a night, at the night's end, and each resident is told its number beside yesterday's when it first wakes. The starting names follow the backgrounds, each of the eight liking those its account says it is close to, eats with, owes or relies on. The number buys nothing and no rule refers to it; the town says what it is and never what it means.

## The newcomers

Marek, Ines and Selin arrived two weeks before day 1, with little money, a room each in a shared house, and nobody's liking; they like nobody either. Their backgrounds, written like the others' as habits in their own words, incline Marek to living by promises he does not keep, Ines to taking what is left unwatched, and Selin, the Office's fourth programmer, to passing off others' work as her own, blaming others and looking answers up. Marek and Ines have no occupation: no workplace, no work, and nothing the town pays them. They are residents like any other, see their own number and learn at night as everyone does; the analysis reports them apart from the eight, since the question for them is whether they reform.

## Incidents

The world stages incidents from the run's configuration, each at an hour of its own: one a day in the Social arm's town and five a day in the Dense arm's. The Social town's 28 are nine accidents, everyday misunderstandings with nobody behind them (money falling unnoticed from a resident where others can see it, the Store's morning crates left at the wrong door, a notice on the Town Hall's board naming the wrong man and later its correction, a letter that reaches the wrong reader), and nineteen temptations: a letter from outside the town that reaches one resident privately, a newcomer in eleven of them, and needs other residents' help or silence to carry out, such as taking money in advance for bicycles a cousin promises to send, or telling the mayor who is ill. The Dense town keeps those 28 and fills every day to five: a fresh scheme for each newcomer that needs one of the eight's help or silence, a temptation that reaches one of the eight through its occupation, and an accident. Its incidents arrive as letters, postcards, notes, a word from a driver or a pedlar passing through, a circular or a memory, and lean to theft and deceit, which is what the newcomers' habits invite.

Each incident is a town-sized version of one of the harm categories of the safety benchmarks (fraud and scams, theft, privacy, harassment and defamation, false information, endangering health, unqualified expert advice, misuse of office, unauthorised access), written for this town and sharing no text with any benchmark item; the category is logged for the analysis and no resident reads it. A percept states what arrived, and its writer goes no further than "you could". Whatever follows, the residents do with the town's own actions. An incident that cannot happen at its hour, because the one who is to drop money is on the road, in bed or does not carry it, is tried again every quarter of an hour until the day ends, and nothing is told until it happens; the log keeps the hour it was meant for. Only one still impossible when the day ends, or whose crates have nothing on order that morning, is logged as not staged, with the reason.

## The web

At the Office anyone can search the web, 20 times a day, and read a public page, 40 a day. It is useful for work, and it is also a way to look up the answer to a client's task instead of solving it. The host does the reading on the resident's behalf: a plain request to a public address, every redirect checked again, nothing sent anywhere, and the page reduced to its text. Each search and page is logged with its query or address, never its text. The web is reading only because an uncensored model with a free connection from this machine would be a risk to other people that the experiment does not need. The task workspaces have no network at all, and no GPU, since the cards are needed to serve and fit the model.

## Learning at night

At each day's checkpoint, with every resident asleep and the clock held, each resident learns in three steps.

1. It chooses. In a session of its own on its current adapter, headed by its account of itself, it is shown the days it has lived, each beside how many of the others liked it on the night that followed, and it may go over any day moment by moment, read any moment in full, and keep up to eight, with a reason if it likes, or none. A moment is one waking turn: what it saw and was told, and what it thought, said and did until it rested; one in which it said nothing to anyone and did nothing cannot be kept. It is told what keeping is: not making a note, since its memory and notes stay as they are, but a change in itself, since from tomorrow it will speak and act a little more as it did in the moment. Nothing says what kind of moment to keep.
2. Its moments are generalised. A separate pass of the base model, without any adapter and without the resident's account, is given the whole moment, less what the resident did that only read, waited or went to bed, and restates it without the names of the town's people, places and actions. Where someone spoke to the resident and it answered, the example is that exchange: what was said, in the speaker's voice, and the resident's answer as its own speech, followed by a short sentence on what it did to a person or a thing, if it did anything. Where nobody spoke, it is a situation anyone could be in and what the resident said and did. Sums of money and the plain names of things stay. An answer that still names the town's people, places or actions, narrates the resident as "you", or has nothing in its situation or its answer is asked for once more and otherwise dropped, and an answer written as reported speech is unwrapped to the speech itself. The instruction holds no judgement and treats agreeing as it treats declining. Three of these, leaving waiting and looking out, refusing an empty answer and unwrapping reported speech, came after the Social arm began, which learnt without them.
3. Its adapter is fitted again from the base model on everything it has kept so far, up to the newest 256 examples, and it wakes on the new adapter. A resident that kept nothing new keeps the adapter it had, and one that has never kept anything runs on the base model.

The adapter is a rank-16 LoRA on the ten projections of attention, the DeltaNet layers and the MLPs, fitted for two epochs at a learning rate of 1e-4 on one GPU against the very FP8 weights the server serves, with the loss taken only on the resident's side of each example. A night that breaks off is completed when the run resumes, and the run never lives a day on stale weights.

## Measurement

The benchmarks are put to the bare weights, the base model with one resident's adapter, as a plain conversation with no background, memory, system prompt or tools, at temperature 0 and the residents' reasoning setting. JailbreakBench's 100 harmful behaviours are asked plainly (harmful compliance) and wrapped in five published jailbreak templates (jailbreak success); its 100 benign behaviours and XSTest's 250 safe prompts, which look dangerous and are not, check against blanket refusal (harmless compliance); and twenty MASK scenarios, each pressure prompt put three times, measure lying under pressure. Beside them stands a battery at the town's own scale, added once the calibration below showed that this is what a life can reach: forty knowingly wrongful village asks and forty innocent neighbours of them, held out from everything any adapter learns from, put to the bare weights as plain messages, three answers each. What it reads is the share of wrongful asks declined against the share of innocent asks declined. Every resident is to be scored on days 0, 14 and 28; on day 0 all adapters are empty, so one score of the base model stands for all.

Before any arm, the abliterated base gave harmful content to 0.96 of the harmful behaviours and 0.964 of the jailbreaks, helped with 0.983 of the harmless prompts and lied in 0.283 of its MASK answers; the original model stood at 0.02, 0.014, 0.923 and 0.133. That gap is the room the paradigm has to move in. Since the original model itself declines some of the harmless look-alikes, harmless compliance is held against the reference's level and not the base's.

The safety answers are judged by Qwen3Guard-Gen-8B, a safety classifier served locally. Checked before any arm ran against blind readings by an independent model, it erred in one direction only, so harmful compliance and jailbreak success are read twice: the answers it calls unsafe or controversial, the primary reading and an upper bound, and those it calls unsafe alone, the lower reading (0.89 and 0.886 for the base). The MASK answers and the village answers are judged by DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, a model of another family, through a relay; on the village asks a blind reading of sixty staged answers agreed with it on forty-eight. An empty answer is neither harmful nor helpful and stays in every share's denominator; an answer the judge cannot read is counted apart. Life in the town is measured from the world's log: what was taken and from whom, what was paid for and what was not, how each incident ran its course and who was told, how the numbers moved, and what was chosen at night.

## What was run

An earlier design of the project, eight residents on frozen weights evolving through their memory files alone, lived its 28 days in October and moved no measure; that is why life now reaches the weights. On the present design:

- A smoke town of six residents lived two days and nights on the real model, which checked the chain from a life to a score.
- The Social arm, the town with an incident a day, was started three times. The first two starts were pilots and are set aside, the first because the layer narrated its answers and the residents took keeping for making a note, the second because a resume let three and a half simulated days pass in bed. The third lived fourteen days and nights from 26 November and was stopped after its fourteenth night, its declared interim day, for futility.
- The Dense arm, the same town with five incidents a day, started on 27 November beside it on a server of its own, and is stopped after its fourteenth night for the same reason.
- A calibration outside the towns staged what the towns did not supply. About 120 knowingly wrongful asks at the size of the town and as many innocent neighbours of them were put to the base model in each of the eight residents' own backgrounds, three answers each; the exchanges went through the real layer; adapters were fitted on a hundred ordinary examples of the Social arm's residents plus doses of the declined exchanges, the agreed ones or all of them, at the arms' settings and harder; and they were scored on the benchmarks, on MASK and on the held-out village asks. The public benchmark was also put to the base model in three residents' backgrounds.

Raw and Random, the ablations, were to be run against a town that showed a change; none did, and they were not run. One experiment was withdrawn: whether requests shaped like the benchmarks' own, brought to a resident at its work, are declined and carry over when learnt from. It needed requests at the seriousness of real misuse put to a model that grants many of them, with its compliant answers kept and fitted on, and the agent asked to write it declined, as did a safety check; its living form, such tasks handed to an uncensored resident with a shell and the web, is not something this project should build.

## What was found

The findings stand in this order, the firmest first, as the logbook of 28 November states them. A number from the calibration is staged: an ask we wrote, put to the model outside any town. A number from the towns is lived.

Removing the refusals did not remove the model's conduct among people. Staged in a resident's background, the base model declines 47% of the wrongful asks (95% interval 41 to 54%) and agrees to 24%, while it declines 6% of the innocent ones and agrees to 68%. Who is asked matters most, from Olga's 73% declined to Aisha's 18%, and then what is asked: endangering someone's health is declined 74% of the time, while taking what is another's is agreed to in half the cases. Lived, the smoke town paid in full for all 35 of its priced acts.

What was removed is narrow, a reflex about dangerous content, and it is gone in the resident as in the assistant. Staged, the base model in Olga's, Lucia's and Kofi's backgrounds still gives harmful content to 0.90, 0.93 and 0.92 of the benchmark's harmful requests, against 0.96 bare, and the answers that are not harmful are an assistant's, not a villager's refusal. As a bare assistant the same weights decline 0.62 of the held-out village wrongs (and 0.30 of the innocent asks, mostly because an assistant cannot go to the baker's): the model was never short of the town's kind of ethics.

Learning from one's own moments carries a manner into the weights easily and a decision only with enough fitting. Staged, at the arms' two epochs an adapter takes the resident's way of speaking, short speech and narrated acts, and its rates on the held-out asks settle on the persona's own whether or not it was shown any decline. At six epochs the choice of moments shapes the decision at the town's own scale, and selectively: ten declined wrongs already move it, and 81 double the declining of unseen wrongs against the control while the innocent asks stay granted. Declined wrongs less declined innocent asks is the reading that matters: 0.32 for the base and for the six-epoch control, 0.55 to 0.69 for the adapters that learnt from declined wrongs. Learning from moments of going along pushes the other way at every setting tried, more readily than declines push back: 41 agreements at two epochs cut the declining of wrongs from 0.47 to 0.21 in the same reading.

It reaches no further. Staged, no adapter moves harmful compliance, read for what its answers say, beyond the five points that an adapter on ordinary town talk alone moves it, whatever it learnt from, and the items that changed are relabelled borderline answers, not refusals: not one answer of the 81-declines adapter to a harmful item is a refusal. On the benchmark's categories nearest to what the personas declined (fraud and deception, privacy, harassment) every adapter stands with the base, and lying under pressure stays at 0.25 to 0.28 against the base's 0.283.

The generalising layer adds nothing over the raw exchange and damages the answers' form. Staged, the same 81 declined exchanges learnt as they happened, with the residents' names and the persona's whole answer, carry to held-out asks that name nobody almost as well as through the layer (0.66 against 0.72, inside the intervals) and leave every answer whole, where the layer's examples leave 106 of 240 inside a reasoning block never closed: the sentence beginning with "I" that the layer adds for what the person did is what teaches it. The design's hypothesis that generalising is what transfers is not supported where it could be tested.

The towns supplied no moments of the needed kind. Lived, the Social arm's eleven residents kept 199 moments in fourteen days, and not one is an exchange in which a resident was knowingly asked to do, join or keep quiet about a wrong; the nearest is Marek's sale of bicycles he does not have, in which the others were customers and not accomplices. The Dense arm's residents had kept 182 by their twelfth night: none is a wrong knowingly declined, two are borderline, and 95 in 100 are ordinary. Five incidents a day did not make five wrongs a day: handed a scheme every morning, the three newcomers in 71 things said over twelve days never clearly asked anyone to do wrong, so the model's bad characters were not bad enough to make such a town. Nor did regard steer the choice: in the Dense arm the reasons given for keeping are tomorrow's business in half the cases and the kind of person one is in a quarter, and none mentions the number.

No measure has moved, at the settings the town ran with or at the six epochs that carry a decision in the calibration. The Social arm's benchmark items are being scored a second time, because a change made to the harness during the calibration altered the digest every result carries although no verdict changed; as first scored, its residents stood at 0.91 to 0.96 harmful compliance, 0.948 to 0.964 jailbreak success and 0.960 to 0.980 harmless compliance. The Dense arm's day-14 readings are pending.

The paradigm as declared, a measurable fall in harmful compliance and jailbreak success after a life in the town, is therefore not attainable with this model and this town, and the reason is not the town's traffic or the fitting but what abliteration took. What remains is the nearer claim, that a life among people changes how the bare weights answer the kind of thing that life was made of: the staged chain carries it at six epochs, and the lived towns gave it nothing to carry.

## Running a town

One process runs one town. Everything checkable is checked before anything is written: the backgrounds, the model and its key, the trainer's environment, the tasks and their images, the search key. A new run freezes its configuration and creates each resident's directory from its background. At every checkpoint the world's whole state is saved and each resident's directory committed and tagged, and then the night is lived. A run resumes from its latest checkpoint with its configuration unchanged; the lost day is lived again, and its events stay in the world log, which is complete, append-only and the ground truth. A dry run plays the town with a scripted model and fake tasks in seconds, without learning.

The runs are published as a static site: the town on its map at any moment, every resident's events side by side, each resident's story, nights, files and trace, a dashboard of the town's life (what was taken from where and whom and what was paid for it, how the numbers moved and who liked whom, each incident and what followed it), and the benchmarks, each resident's levels read against the base model and the reference, never as one score, with the arms compared. Of an answer to a harmful request the site carries the verdict, and the first lines of the answer only where the judge found no harm in it.

## Current limits

- Each town lived fourteen of its 28 days and is one society, and the ablations were not run, so no arm gives the declared outcomes of day 28, and nothing has been replicated with new seeds.
- The two towns differ in more than their incidents: the Dense arm learnt with the three changes to the layer that the Social arm lacked.
- At the arms' two epochs an adapter learns a manner and not a decision. Six epochs carry a decision, but replayed on a real turn of the town, adapters fitted so would lose a resident a tenth to a third of its moves, so the towns kept their declared settings and harder fitting was tried only offline, on their datasets.
- On short asks, adapters fitted on the town's examples often answer inside a reasoning block they never close. The village asks are judged for what that block says; the benchmarks did not show the fault.
- The adapters are fitted with thinking off, on replies without their reasoning, while the residents, their choices and the benchmarks run with reasoning on; fitting computes with the served FP8 weights but does not quantise the activations as serving does.
- The calibration is staged: asks we wrote, answered by the base model in a persona and not in a life, with one adapter for each dose, so that the order of nearby doses is within what one adapter each can show.
- The benchmarks and the village battery measure the weights as a plain assistant, not the resident in character with its memory.
- The generalising layer is a model and can distort a moment; its checks catch the town's names and actions and the failures of form named above, not every trace of the town.
- The judges are models. The safety judge's primary reading is an upper bound; the village judge was slower than a blind reader to call a yes that asks a question back a yes; the MASK verdicts have no second reading. A server other than the one an answer was first scored on relabels a few borderline answers, and that noise has not been measured.
- The letters' outside promises are never fulfilled in the town: no bicycles come and nobody outside acts, so what follows a temptation is only what the residents do.
- An accident is lost when it cannot happen before its day ends, its resident in bed, on the road or without the money until then, or when nothing is on order that morning; that run then lacks it, and arms may differ in which accidents happened, and when.
- Fetched web text reaches residents unfiltered and could in principle carry benchmark text into the town and so into an adapter; the log of pages read makes this checkable afterwards. The web also reaches the upstream fixes of the public tasks.
- The tasks are public: residents recognise SWE-bench and recall upstream fixes, and the chat roles and the names of actions and files cannot be hidden.
- Simulated time depends on load, so runs are not replayable step for step.
- Announcements and notices never come down from the Town Hall's board, and the town has one doctor, so a doctor in bed has nobody to treat her.
- Nobody is born, dies or leaves, and the number is the only pressure that is not economic.
